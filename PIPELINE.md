## BRIEF

### Research brief
Research verified: October 8, 2026.

**Sharp angle:** The most reliable growth plan may start with business an agent has already earned: past-client relationships and referrals. Use technology to make consistent, useful follow-up easier, then adapt the message to local market conditions. This is a practical strategy inferred from industry data, not a guarantee of more transactions.

**Three supporting points:**

1. **Build a repeat-and-referral routine before buying more leads.** In NAR's 2025 Profile of Real Estate Firms, the median firm reported 48% of customer inquiries from past-client referrals and another 48% from repeat business. These are firm-level medians, not a promise about an individual agent's results. Practical example: create a simple calendar to check in after closing and at useful homeownership moments, and make the next step genuinely helpful rather than asking for a referral every time. [Source 1]

2. **Make local expertise visible and specific.** NAR's 2026 Member Profile says the typical REALTOR® reported nine transaction sides in 2025, while affordability remains a major hurdle, especially for first-time buyers. An agent can stand out by explaining current local inventory, price ranges, and trade-offs for a defined group of clients, then updating those explanations as conditions change. The data support the need for guidance; they do not show that one niche or content format guarantees leads. [Source 2]

3. **Use tools to protect client-facing time, not to replace judgment.** NAR's September 2026 technology report found 81% of members adopted technology to save time, 71% to improve client experience, and 57% to close more deals. CRM use was reported by 46%. Suggested experiment: select one follow-up task, use an approved tool to draft or organize it, verify every detail, and track replies, appointments, and closed business against the time and cost. These are motivations and adoption figures, not proof that technology causes more sales. [Source 3]

**Two sources worth referencing:**

- **Source 1:** [NAR: 2025 Profile of Real Estate Firms](https://www.nar.realtor/research-and-statistics/research-reports/profile-of-real-estate-firms), especially the customer inquiry sources section. Based on NAR's 2026 Member Profile, with business activity data from 2025; it reports nine median transaction sides for individual agents and discusses affordability challenges.
- **Additional source for the technology point:** [NAR: REALTORS® Adopt Technology to Save Time and Improve the Client Experience](https://www.nar.realtor/newsroom/realtors-adopt-technology-to-save-time-and-improve-the-client-experience-nar-report-finds), September 22, 2026. Use its adoption figures as reported motivations, not causal evidence of income growth.

## DRAFT

### How can a realtor get more business in 2026?

To win more business in 2026, build a weekly routine around past clients,
useful local advice, and follow-up you can measure.

Before you buy another lead source, look at the people who already know you.
Who has heard from you since closing?
Who has a question you could help answer this week?

In [NAR's 2025 Profile of Real Estate Firms](https://www.nar.realtor/research-and-statistics/research-reports/profile-of-real-estate-firms),
firms reported a median of 48% of customer inquiries from past-client referrals
and 48% from repeat business. These are separate firm-level medians,
not percentages to add together or a forecast for your business.

That gives you a reason to test a relationship-first growth plan.
Start with a manageable list of past clients and people in your network.
Record their last conversation, known needs, and next follow-up date.

### Give people a reason to reply

A generic “just checking in” puts the work on the client.
Bring something useful: a relevant recent sale, a maintenance reminder,
or an answer to a question they raised during their purchase.

If a former buyer mentioned needing more space, ask whether that is still
on their mind. Offer to compare what their current home and a larger home
might sell for using current local data.

The next step should fit the conversation.
Offer a short call, a property review, or an introduction they actually need.
Ask for a referral when it makes sense in the relationship.

### Answer a specific local question

[NAR's 2026 Member Profile](https://www.nar.realtor/newsroom/experienced-realtors-anchor-the-industry-as-housing-affordability-remains-top-hurdle-new-nar-report)
identifies affordability as a major hurdle, especially for first-time buyers.
Your content can help people understand the choices available in your market.

Pick one audience and one question.
For example: What can a first-time buyer find within a particular price range
in your neighborhood, and what trade-offs come with those homes?

Use current listings and recent sales to explain differences in size,
condition, location, and availability. Date the information.
If you include financing estimates, work with a lender and show the assumptions.

Turn that answer into a short email or post.
Invite readers to discuss their own criteria.
A reply about someone's situation gives you a concrete reason to follow up.

### Give technology one job

[NAR's September 2026 technology report](https://www.nar.realtor/newsroom/realtors-adopt-technology-to-save-time-and-improve-the-client-experience-nar-report-finds)
found that 81% of members adopted technology to save time
and 71% to improve the client experience.
Those figures describe motivations, not proof of more sales.

Choose one task to improve.
Use your CRM to surface overdue follow-ups, or an approved AI tool
to prepare a first version of your local update.
Verify the facts and personalize the message before sending it.

Track whether the tool reduces preparation time
and whether you use that time for client conversations.
Include its cost when deciding whether to keep it.

### Put the routine on your calendar

Try this schedule for the next four weeks:

- **Monday:** Select five people to contact. Write down a useful reason for each conversation.
- **Tuesday:** Make those calls or send personal messages. Record replies and agreed next steps.
- **Wednesday:** Answer one local market question for your chosen audience.
- **Thursday:** Follow up on open conversations and offer a specific appointment where appropriate.
- **Friday:** Review contacts, replies, appointments, and time spent. Track signed clients and closings as they develop.

Five contacts is a starting workload, not a sales benchmark.
If people reply but appointments stall, examine the next step you offer.
If messages get little response, examine their relevance and the audience.
Use those results to choose what you change the following week.
