## NEXT UP

- How can a realtor get more business in 2026?

## BRIEF

### Research brief
Research verified: October 8, 2026.

**Sharp angle:** The most reliable growth plan may start with business an agent has already earned: past-client relationships and referrals. Use technology to make consistent, useful follow-up easier, then adapt the message to local market conditions. This is a practical strategy inferred from industry data, not a guarantee of more transactions.

**Three supporting points:**

1. **Build a repeat-and-referral routine before buying more leads.** In NAR's 2025 Profile of Real Estate Firms, the median firm reported 48% of customer inquiries from past-client referrals and another 48% from repeat business. These are firm-level medians, not a promise about an individual agent's results. Practical example: create a simple calendar to check in after closing and at useful homeownership moments, and make the next step genuinely helpful rather than asking for a referral every time. [Source 1]

2. **Make local expertise visible and specific.** NAR's 2026 Member Profile says the typical REALTOR® reported nine transaction sides in 2025, while affordability remains a major hurdle, especially for first-time buyers. An agent can stand out by explaining current local inventory, price ranges, and trade-offs for a defined group of clients, then updating those explanations as conditions change. The data support the need for guidance; they do not show that one niche or content format guarantees leads. [Source 2]

3. **Use tools to protect client-facing time, not to replace judgment.** NAR's September 2026 technology report found 81% of members adopted technology to save time, 71% to improve client experience, and 57% to close more deals. CRM use was reported by 46%. Suggested experiment: select one follow-up task, use an approved tool to draft or organize it, verify every detail, and track replies, appointments, and closed business against the time and cost. These are motivations and adoption figures, not proof that technology causes more sales. [Source 3]

**Two sources worth referencing:**

- **Source 1:** [NAR: 2025 Profile of Real Estate Firms](https://www.nar.realtor/research-and-statistics/research-reports/profile-of-real-estate-firms), especially the customer inquiry sources section. Firm-level median distribution: 48% past-client referrals and 48% repeat business from past clients. Keep the firm-level denominator clear.
- **Source 2:** [NAR: Experienced REALTORS® Anchor the Industry as Housing Affordability Remains Top Hurdle](https://www.nar.realtor/newsroom/experienced-realtors-anchor-the-industry-as-housing-affordability-remains-top-hurdle-new-nar-report), June 25, 2026. Based on NAR's 2026 Member Profile, with business activity data from 2025; it reports nine median transaction sides for individual agents and discusses affordability challenges.
- **Additional source for the technology point:** [NAR: REALTORS® Adopt Technology to Save Time and Improve the Client Experience](https://www.nar.realtor/newsroom/realtors-adopt-technology-to-save-time-and-improve-the-client-experience-nar-report-finds), September 22, 2026. Use its adoption figures as reported motivations, not causal evidence of income growth.

**One audience question:** How can I keep finding clients when buyers are worried about affordability? This is a paraphrase of the affordability concern highlighted in NAR's 2026 Member Profile, not a verbatim consumer quote.

**Writer handoff:** Give readers a concrete weekly system: care for the existing client network, offer useful local expertise to a defined audience, and measure whether consistent follow-up produces conversations and appointments. Separate suggestions from survey findings; do not promise a certain number of leads, transactions, or income.
